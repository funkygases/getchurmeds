I
Act One
NeuroCity — General Design, Synapses, Second Messengers & Transmitters
Scene 01
General Design · Synapses
The City of NeuroCity & Its Postal Service
▾
NeuroCity is a vast metropolis with three departments: the Sensory Bureau (information flows inward from the outskirts), the Motor Division (commands flow outward to the factories and muscle contractors), and the Autonomic Agency — a shadow department that runs everything automatically without needing to be asked.

Every message in the city travels neuron to neuron via the Postal Service. There are two kinds of post offices. The Chemical Post Office has a tiny gap — the synaptic cleft — between the sender (Pre) and the receiver (Post). Pre packages chemical envelopes (neurotransmitters) and tosses them across the 200–300 Å gap. Post catches them at receptor slots on its door. The message can excite, inhibit, or change Post's sensitivity. The Electrical Post Office is different — there's no gap at all. Pre and Post share a back tunnel called a gap junction (only 20–40 Å). Ions walk directly through. No package needed. These tunnels exist only in the smooth muscle and cardiac districts.
⭐ High-Yield Chemical synapse = neurotransmitter across cleft (200–300 Å). Electrical synapse = gap junction, direct ion flow (20–40 Å), no transmitter. Gap junctions: smooth muscle + cardiac muscle only.

The neuron building itself: The main office is the Soma (cell body). The long hallway leading out is the Axon (conducts the signal out to peripheral nerves). The Dendrites are the branching mailboxes on the front of the building — they receive incoming signals, but most can't fire action potentials on their own (too few voltage-gated Na⁺ channels, threshold too high). The Cerebral Cortex is the city archive — where most memories are stored. Facilitation: when a route gets used repeatedly, the Post Office on that route gets faster and more efficient — eventually it can fire on its own even without an incoming message.

Mnemonic · Neuron Parts SAD-CF — Soma, Axon, Dendrites, Cortex (memory), Facilitation

Scene 02
Second Messengers · G Protein
The G Protein Heist
▾
Deep inside the postsynaptic office, a sleeping crew of three — Alpha (α), Beta (β), and Gamma (γ) — are chained together in the lobby, holding an old ID card called GDP. They're inactive. Dormant.

Then a neurotransmitter arrives at the front door and the receptor shape-shifts — the conformational change unlocks Alpha's cage. Alpha rips up the GDP card, grabs a new one called GTP, and breaks away from Beta and Gamma. Now Alpha-GTP is free to roam the building and does one of four things:
Mnemonic · G Protein's 4 Downstream Effects "ICON"
  • I — Ion channel opens in the postsynaptic membrane (e.g., K⁺)
  • C — cAMP / cGMP activated → metabolic machinery fired up
  • O — One or more intracellular enzymes activated
  • N — New proteins formed via gene transcription
Alpha's rampage ends when someone hydrolizes his GTP card back to GDP. Alpha shuffles back to Beta and Gamma, they reconnect — and the whole crew falls asleep again. That's G protein inactivation.
ResponseWhat Opens / ClosesNet Effect
ExcitationNa⁺ channels open; Cl⁻/K⁺ depressedMembrane potential ↑ toward positive
InhibitionCl⁻ channels open; K⁺ exitsIncreased negativity inside neuron
⭐ High-Yield Excitation = Na⁺ flooding in (positive). Inhibition = Cl⁻ flooding in OR K⁺ flooding out (more negative). Inhibition can occur pre- OR post-synaptically.

Scene 03
Transmission Modifiers · Summation
The City's Weather & Traffic Report
▾
NeuroCity's signal network is sensitive to weather and traffic. The city dispatcher watches the conditions and adjusts everything accordingly.
Mnemonic · Transmission Modifiers "FAHD-C"
  • F — Fatigue (transmitter stores depleted → ↓ transmission)
  • A — Acidosis depresses; Alkalosis excites ("Acid puts the city to sleep")
  • H — Hypoxia depresses neuronal activity
  • D — Drugs: Caffeine/theophylline excite; Anesthetics depress
  • C — Convergence / Summation (spatial = more lanes; temporal = higher frequency)
⭐ CRNA Pearl Anesthetics work by depressing neuronal excitability. Acidosis + hypoxia also depress — this explains altered mental status when patients are acidotic or hypoxic perioperatively. Alkalosis excites — think about hyperventilation-induced seizure risk.

Scene 04
Transmitters · Neurotransmitters
The City's Courier Corps
▾
NeuroCity has two classes of couriers. The Small-Molecule Couriers are fast, efficient workers made in the presynaptic terminal, packed into recyclable vesicles, and reused over and over. The Neuropeptide Couriers are large, powerful VIP messengers made in the cell body itself by ribosomes — they ship in single-use containers that are destroyed after delivery. They're slower but far more potent and their effects last longer.
Mnemonic · The 8 Small-Molecule Couriers "ANGRY DOGS NO"
  • A — Acetylcholine (motor, ANS, everywhere)
  • N — Norepinephrine (brainstem, hypothalamus, post-gang SNS)
  • G — Glutamate (primary excitatory — sensory pathways + CNS)
  • R — (sero)tonin — brain, spinal cord, hypothalamus, mood + pain mod
  • Y — (glYcine) — spinal cord, inhibitory
  • D — Dopamine (substantia nigra — motor control)
  • O — (nitric Oxide) — brain, retrograde messenger
  • G — GABA (primary inhibitory — everywhere: cord, cerebellum, basal ganglia, cortex)
  • S — (no letter needed — just: GABA & Glutamate are the two most important)
⭐ Must Know GABA = primary CNS inhibitory transmitter. Glutamate = primary CNS excitatory transmitter. Dopamine from substantia nigra — lose it → Parkinson's. Substance P + Glutamate = slow and fast pain transmitters. Neuropeptide vesicles are autolyzed, NOT recycled.

Scene 05
CNS Levels
The Three Floors of City Hall
▾
NeuroCity Hall has three floors. Floor 1 (Spinal Cord): the ground floor janitors — they handle reflexes, walking, blood vessels, GI, urinary control, and they run messages between the streets and upstairs. They work with or without direction from above. Floor 2 (Subcortical — medulla, pons, hypothalamus, thalamus, cerebellum, basal ganglia): middle management — blood pressure, equilibrium, salivation, emotions. Floor 3 (Cortex): the executive suite and memory archive. But here's the rule: the executive suite NEVER works alone. It always operates through the floors below.
⭐ Key Concept The cortex is where memory is stored — but it NEVER functions alone. It always works in association with lower brain centers. This is why cortical lesions don't necessarily abolish all function.

Act Two
The Road Network — Receptors, Nerve Fibers & Sensory Pathways
Scene 06
Sensory Receptors · Adaptation
The City's Five Intelligence Agencies
▾
On the outskirts of NeuroCity, five intelligence agencies monitor the world and report inward. Each specializes in a different threat category.
Mnemonic · 5 Receptor Types "MTNEC"
"Mechanics, Thermostats, Nosy Cops (pain), Electricians (light), Chemists"
  • M — Mechanoreceptors (pressure, stretch, touch)
  • T — Thermoreceptors (temperature change)
  • N — Nociceptors (pain — free nerve endings; adapt poorly)
  • E — Electromagnetic (light on retina)
  • C — Chemoreceptors (taste, smell, O₂, CO₂, osmolality)

Adaptation: All agencies eventually get bored of a constant signal and slow their reporting — until they stop almost entirely. The pain agency (Nociceptors) is famously bad at adapting — they never really shut up. That's why pain persists. It's also why hyperalgesia occurs — their sensitivity keeps ramping up instead of down. Tonic receptors are the slow-adapting ones: pain, baroreceptors, vestibular macula, carotid/aortic body chemoreceptors.

The agencies send their reports along the road network — but not all roads are the same.
Scene 07
Nerve Fiber Types · Differential Blockade
The Highway System & The Blockade
▾
NeuroCity's road network has strict classifications. The biggest, fastest highways are Aα — 12–20 μm wide, heavily insulated (myelinated), highest speed limit. They carry motor commands and proprioception. Then the network grades down through Aβ, Aγ, and Aδ — each smaller and slower. On the far edge of town, the C fiber back alleys — unmyelinated, barely 0.3–1.2 μm wide, crawling speed. They carry the slow aching pain, itch, crude touch, and are also the postganglionic autonomic routes.

When the Anesthetic Fog rolls in (neuraxial blockade), it closes the toll booths in order — smallest roads first, biggest last.
Mnemonic · Neuraxial Blockade ORDER (first → last blocked) "Big Cats Always Go Anywhere"
  • B — B fibers (preganglionic sympathetic efferents) — FIRST blocked
  • C — C & Aδ (pain, temp, touch afferents; post-gang sympathetic)
  • Aγ — A-gamma (muscle spindle)
  • G→Aβ — A-beta (touch + pressure)
  • Aα — A-alpha (motor + proprioception) — LAST blocked
⭐ Differential Blockade Rule Sympathetic block: 2–5 segments ABOVE sensory block. Sensory block: 2–5 segments ABOVE motor block. Smaller fibers = more easily blocked. The further the local anesthetic is from the nerve, the less blockade.

Scene 08
Sensory Pathways · Somatosensory Cortex · Dermatomes
The Two Couriers to the Penthouse
▾
Every signal entering the spinal cord at the dorsal root must reach the Cortex Penthouse. There are exactly two express routes — and they're completely different in how they get there.

Courier One: The Dorsal Column Express (DC Metro) — takes the fast lane up the same side of the cord all the way to the Medulla Bridge, where it finally switches sides (2nd-order neuron crosses), then rides the Medial Lemniscus Elevated Line to the Thalamus Hotel, and finally the 3rd-order neuron runs to the Cortex Penthouse. This courier carries fine touch, vibration, joint position, pressure.

Courier Two: The Anterolateral Alley (Spinothalamic) — enters the dorsal root, immediately cuts across at the spinal cord level via the anterior commissure (no waiting), then rides up the front alleys to the brain stem (reticular nuclei) and Thalamus Hotel. This courier carries pain, temperature, crude touch, tickle/itch, sexual sensations.
Mnemonic · Where Each Pathway Crosses "Dorsal = Delayed (crosses at Medulla). Anterior = At the Address (crosses at cord level immediately)"
  • Dorsal Column → crosses at Medulla (far up, delayed)
  • Anterolateral → crosses At spinal cord level (immediately)
⭐ Why This Matters: Brown-Séquard If the left cord is cut: motor and dorsal column (proprioception/vibration) are lost ipsilaterally (left side, below). Pain and temperature are lost contralaterally (right side, 2–6 dermatomes below). Because the spinothalamic crossed immediately at cord level, and the dorsal column hadn't crossed yet.

At the Penthouse (Somatosensory Cortex): The Central Sulcus is the building's dividing wall — sensory floors are behind it (postcentral gyrus), motor floors are in front. Somatosensory Area I (Brodmann's 3, 1, 2) handles precise body localization but NOT pain or temperature. Dermatomes: every spinal nerve owns a strip of skin — but the borders overlap.

Mnemonic · Brodmann's Areas for SSA I "3-1-2 = Three-One-Two = Somatosensory Area I" — behind the central sulcus, postcentral gyrus. No pain, no temp.

Scene 09
Pain Types · Causes · Transmission
Two Detectives & Their Cases
▾
The Pain District has two detectives on the beat. Detective Aδ — fast, sharp, electric baton. Fires within 0.1 seconds. Sharp, pricking, acute. Handles mechanical and thermal cases. Transmits via the Neospinothalamic Pathway — straight to lamina I, across anterolateral column, up to thalamus and cortex. Neurotransmitter: Glutamate (fast delivery).

Detective C — slow burner. Doesn't show up until 1+ second after the incident. Aching, throbbing, chronic. Handles tissue destruction cases — mechanical, thermal, and chemical. Takes the Paleospinothalamic Pathway — lands in lamina II & III (Substantia Gelatinosa), passes through lamina V, then rides anterolateral up to the brain stem reticular nuclei, tectal area, and the Periaqueductal Gray. Neurotransmitters: Glutamate + Substance P.
Mnemonic · Chemical Causes of Pain "BISHOP PEAKS"
  • B — Bradykinin
  • I — Ions (K⁺ ions)
  • S — Serotonin
  • H — Histamine
  • O — (proteOlytic enzymes)
  • P — Prostaglandins + Substance P (sensitizers — enhance receptor sensitivity)
  • K — (acidsK+ — lactic acid in ischemia)
  • A — Acetylcholine
⭐ Key Distinction Bradykinin, serotonin, histamine, K⁺, acetylcholine, acids, proteolytic enzymes = excite pain receptors. Prostaglandins + Substance P = enhance sensitivity (sensitizers). Ischemia pain = lactic acid + bradykinin + proteolytic enzymes.
The city has its own police chief who can suppress the pain detectives.
Scene 10
Analgesia System · Referred Pain
The Police Chief & The Crossed Wires
▾
High in the city, in the Periaqueductal Gray Tower, sits the Police Chief — the head of the endogenous analgesia department. When the Chief activates, she sends orders down through the Raphe Magnus Dispatch Center, down the dorsolateral columns, to a Pain Inhibitory Precinct in the dorsal horn. This precinct can block pain signals before they ever reach the Cortex Penthouse. Morphine and opioid drugs work by impersonating the Chief's orders.

The Chief's messengers are: Enkephalin, Endorphins, and Serotonin.
Mnemonic · Endogenous Analgesia Transmitters "EES" — Enkephalin, Endorphins, Serotonin
  • PAG Tower → Raphe Magnus → Dorsolateral columns → Dorsal horn inhibitory precinct
The Crossed Wires (Referred Pain): In the basement of the dorsal horn dispatch, the visceral organ wires and the skin wires accidentally share the same 2nd-order neuron terminal. When the heart or stomach fires a pain signal, the brain upstairs can't tell if it came from the organ or from the skin — so it blames the skin. The brain blames the dermatomal address where that organ grew up embryologically. The heart started in the neck and upper thorax → pain referred to C3–T5.
⭐ Referred Pain Rule Pain referred to the dermatomal segment from which the organ originated embryologically — NOT necessarily where it sits now. Heart: C3–T5. Visceral pain via C fibers → only chronic, aching (not sharp).
Mnemonic · Causes of Referred Pain "ISCO" — Ischemia, Spasm (hollow viscus), Chemical stimuli, Overdistention

Scene 12
Vision · Eye Anatomy · Periop Complications
The Optical Bureau & Its Plumbing Problems
▾
The Optical Bureau is a pressurized building. The Aqueous Humor is a freely flowing liquid maintained at ~15 mmHg by constant formation and drainage through the Canal of Schlemm (the drain pipe). If the drain clogs — that's Glaucoma. Pressure builds, compresses the optic nerve at the disc, and vision dies. Behind the lens, Vitreous Humor is the gel that holds the back wall (retina) in place. The retina's inner layers are fed by the central retinal artery; the outer layers by the choroid.
⭐ Periop Visual Complications Corneal Abrasion = most common periop ophthalmic complication. Prevention: tape eyelids shut. Dx: fluorescein stain + cobalt blue light.

Posterior ION = more common perioperatively. Risk factors:

Act Four
The Disorders — When NeuroCity Breaks Down
Scene 14
Alzheimer & Parkinson Disease
The Crumbling Archive & The Dopamine Blackout
▾
The Crumbling Archive (Alzheimer's): The City Archive (cortex) is slowly filling with sticky plaque — amyloid beta-rich deposits — and the filing systems are collapsing into tangles (neurofibrillary tangles). Workers can't find records, can't follow instructions. The most common demolition project in the city. Patients are often confused and uncooperative — know this for consent and perioperative care.

The Dopamine Blackout (Parkinson's): In the Motor District's power plant — the Substantia Nigra — the dopamine generators fail. Without dopamine, the inhibitory brake on extrapyramidal motor neurons is gone. The ACh union takes over unchecked, firing constantly. The result: tremor, rigidity, akinesia — the city's motor workers are shaking, stiff, and can't start movement.
Mnemonic · Parkinson's Classic Triad "TRA" — Tremor, Rigidity, Akinesia
  • Treatment: ↑ Dopamine (levodopa, agonists, MAO-B inhibitors, COMT inhibitors)
  • OR ↓ ACh effects (anticholinergics)
Story Thread → connects forward to Scene 16 Parkinson's is a top risk factor for postoperative delirium (POD). The dopamine-ACh imbalance is already fragile — anesthesia, anticholinergics, and the stress of surgery can tip it into full delirium.
Scene 15
Seizures · Status Epilepticus
The City-Wide Power Surge
▾
A seizure is a transient, paroxysmal, synchronous misfiring of a group of neurons — like a neighborhood's electrical grid surging simultaneously. If it's one neighborhood only, that's a Focal Seizure. If the surge spreads to both sides of the city — Generalized (Tonic-Clonic) — the airway district goes offline. Status Epilepticus is when the surge never stops — a life-threatening emergency.
Mnemonic · Seizure Classification "Simple = Same side, Sober. Complex = Confusion. Generalized = Grab the airway."
  • Simple Partial — focal, 1 hemisphere, consciousness intact
  • Complex Partial — focal, 1 hemisphere, consciousness impaired
  • Generalized Tonic-Clonic — both hemispheres, airway often compromised
⚠ Status Epilepticus = Emergency IV access + pharmacologic suppression (benzodiazepines first) + airway support. Don't forget the airway — generalized seizures compromise it.
Scene 16
POD · POCD · Perioperative Neurocognitive Disorders
Post-Surgery NeuroCity: The Fog & The Slow Fade
▾
After a major reconstruction project (surgery), NeuroCity sometimes suffers two kinds of aftershocks. The Fog (POD — Postoperative Delirium): an acute storm that hits within hours to days — perception, memory, attention, and consciousness all go haywire simultaneously. Can be hypoactive (quiet, withdrawn) or hyperactive (agitated). Diagnosed by the CAM method. The Slow Fade (POCD): barely noticeable at first — memory quietly slips, concentration drifts, psychomotor speed drags. Starts weeks to months after surgery. Much harder to catch.
Mnemonic · CAM Criteria for Delirium (need 1+2, plus 3 or 4) "AIDA" — Acute onset, Inattention, Disorganized thinking, Altered consciousness
  • A — Acute onset + fluctuating course (1) ✓ Required
  • I — Inattention (2) ✓ Required
  • D — Disorganized thinking (3) — one of these two needed
  • A — Altered level of consciousness (4) — one of these two needed
⭐ Strongest POD Risk Factor Preexisting cognitive impairment or dementia. Age >65 is also major.
Mnemonic · POD Risk Factor Groups "DEMAND EPIC"
  • D — Dementia / cognitive impairment (strongest)
  • E — EEG suppression, ETOH abuse, Electrolyte abnormalities
  • M — Male gender, Metabolic disease
  • A — Age >65, Anticholinergics intraop, Albumin low
  • N — Neurodegenerative (Parkinson's), Nutrition/dehydration
  • D — Depression, Duration of anesthesia
  • E — (poor functional status)
  • P — Pain (inadequate control), Poor functional status
  • I — ICU admission
  • C — CV disease, Certain surgeries (aortic + orthopedic highest)
⭐ POCD vs. POD POD: acute, hours-days, dramatic. POCD: subtle, weeks-months. POCD requires pre + post neurocognitive testing — no universal criteria. Surgery and anesthesia are unlikely to cause persistent dementia — evidence unclear.
Scene 17
Depression · Guillain-Barré Syndrome
The Depleted Couriers & The Insulation Crisis
▾
The Depleted Couriers (Depression): The mood district runs on two couriers — Norepinephrine and Serotonin. When their levels drop, the whole district goes dim. Treatment: drugs that put those couriers back on the streets — SSRIs, TCAs, MAOIs. Each class does it differently, but the goal is the same: more NE and serotonin at the synapse.

The Insulation Crisis (Guillain-Barré): A rare, autoimmune attack strips the myelin insulation off peripheral nerve wires throughout the city — starting at the feet, creeping cephalad. The city loses feeling, then movement, from the legs upward. Intercostal wires fail — ventilation compromised. Pharyngeal wires fail — swallowing compromised. The autonomic system goes haywire: BP spikes, diaphoresis, tachycardia. And — most critically — the exposed wire endings start growing extra ACh receptors everywhere (upregulation). Bring in succinylcholine? It triggers a catastrophic K⁺ surge. The city explodes.
⚠ GBS — AVOID SUCCINYLCHOLINE Denervation → upregulation of ACh receptors across entire muscle membrane → succinylcholine causes life-threatening hyperkalemia. This is an absolute contraindication in GBS.
Mnemonic · GBS Clinical Features "MVSAT"
  • M — Motor: ascending weakness/paralysis, legs → cephalad
  • V — Ventilation: intercostal + pharyngeal weakness → may need vent
  • S — Sensory: paresthesia + pain
  • A — Autonomic: BP swings, diaphoresis, tachycardia, vasoconstriction
  • T — Thromboembolism risk (DVT prophylaxis needed)
Mnemonic · GBS Diagnosis (required features) "PA" — Progressive bilateral weakness + Areflexia
  • Supporting CSF finding: elevated protein + cell count <10/mm³ ("albuminocytologic dissociation")
  • Depression: ↓ NE and/or serotonin → SSRIs / TCAs / MAOIs
