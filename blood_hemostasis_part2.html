PART 2 — Blood & Hemostasis
PT.2 · 1 Erythrocytes, Blood O₂ Content & CO₂ Content
▾
Erythrocytes
  • Biconcave discs, diameter ~7.8 μm — easily change shape to squeeze through small capillaries
  • Circulate ~120 days; primarily removed by spleen
  • Produced in bone marrow from pluripotent hematopoietic stem cells
  • Erythropoietin stimulates production — 90% from kidneys, 10% from liver
  • Renal failure → anemia (failed erythropoietin production)
  • Requirements for RBC production: Vitamin B12, folic acid, and iron
  • Contain carbonic anhydrase — catalyzes CO₂ + H₂O → carbonic acid
High Yield — Blood O₂ Calculations (EXAM)
Bound O₂ (mL/dL)
Hgb × 1.34 × O₂ sat = mL O₂/dL
Example: Hgb 12, sat 98% → 12 × 1.34 × 0.98 = 15.76 mL/dL
Practice: Hgb 14.0, sat 95% → 14 × 1.34 × 0.95 = 17.83 mL/dL
Dissolved O₂ (mL/dL)
PaO₂ × 0.003 = mL O₂/dL
Example: PaO₂ 88 → 88 × 0.003 = 0.26 mL/dL
Total O₂ Content
Bound O₂ + Dissolved O₂
🧠 40-50-60 / 70-80-90 Rule

O₂ sat 70% → PaO₂ ~40 | sat 80% → PaO₂ ~50 | sat 90% → PaO₂ ~60

High Yield — CO₂ Calculations

CO₂ is >20× more soluble in blood than O₂.

Dissolved CO₂ (mL/dL)
PaCO₂ × 0.067 = mL CO₂/dL
Example: PaCO₂ 40 → 40 × 0.067 = 2.68 mL/dL
ETCO₂ → PaCO₂ Relationship
ETCO₂ ≈ PaCO₂ − 5 (normal healthy patient)
In severe respiratory dysfunction, gradient may be 10 or greater

PT.2 · 2 Allowable Blood Loss & Transfusion Decisions
▾
High Yield — Allowable Blood Loss Formula
ABL Formula
ABL = EBV × (Starting Hct − Acceptable Hct) / Starting Hct
Example: 50 kg male → EBV = 75 mL/kg × 50 = 3750 mL; Starting Hct 33, Acceptable 27
→ ABL = 3750 × (33−27)/33 = 682 mL
  • Acceptable Hct: typically 25–30% — lower for healthy young patients, higher for cardiac disease
  • Hct ≈ Hgb × 3 (e.g., Hgb 10 = Hct ~30%)
  • Average EBV: Premature infant 95 | Neonate 85 | Infant 80 | Adult male 75 | Adult female 65 mL/kg — Use IBW if obese
  • Blood loss estimation: 4×4 gauze saturated ~10 mL | Lap sponge ~75–150 mL
High Yield — Transfusion Triggers
Hgb LevelDecision
>10 g/dLTransfusion rarely needed
6–10 g/dLClinical judgment — hemodynamics, CV status, O₂ sat, estimated blood loss. Generally transfuse when Hgb <7 (healthy); higher threshold with CAD/prior MI
<6 g/dLTransfusion almost always indicated
<5 g/dLIncreased mortality
Exam Tip

If asked: the MAIN REASON to transfuse blood products = OXYGEN CARRYING CAPACITY. No single lab value indicates transfusion — always look at multiple factors.

Coagulopathy & Massive Transfusion
  • PRBCs + crystalloids alone do NOT replace coagulation factors — dilutional coagulopathy
  • Severe bleeding requires replacement of coagulation factors & platelets (crystalloids/PRBCs exacerbate coagulopathy)
  • Hypothermia & acidosis common with massive transfusion — also complicate bleeding
  • Microvascular bleeding with plt <50,000 usually requires replacement; >100,000 rarely does
  • FFP for: urgent warfarin reversal, factor deficiencies when specific concentrates unavailable, PT/PTT >1.5× normal + bleeding
  • Cryoprecipitate for: vWD unresponsive to DDAVP or current bleeding, fibrinogen <100 mg/dL
  • Monitor coagulopathy: PTT, PT/INR, platelet count, fibrinogen, TEG

PT.2 · 3 Autologous Transfusion — 3 Categories
▾
1. Preoperative Autologous Donation
  • Collection and storage of patient's own blood over several weeks prior to surgery
  • Up to 3 units; stored ≤35 days as long as Hgb remains >11
  • Last unit donated at least 72 hours prior to surgery
  • Diminishes risk of bacteremia, hemolytic/allergenic/antigenic reactions
  • Indicated for expected blood loss ≥10% in patients healthy enough to tolerate slight anemia
  • Do not transfuse just to prevent waste
2. Acute Normovolemic Hemodilution (ANH)
  • Extract several units of blood prior to incision (before or after induction)
  • Replace volume with appropriate colloid or crystalloid
  • This blood has NORMAL hematocrit and NORMAL clotting factors
  • Blood lost during surgery has LOWER hematocrit (diluted by crystalloid/colloid)
  • Blood re-transfused at end of procedure
3. Intraoperative Blood Salvage (Cell Saver)
  • Collection of shed surgical blood — filtered, washed, re-infused at end of procedure
  • Blood suctioned + anticoagulant (heparin or citrate) → filtered → removes anticoagulant, debris, platelets, WBCs
  • Risks: hemolysis, contamination, dilutional coagulopathy (does NOT contain clotting factors or platelets)
  • Consider for expected blood loss >1,000 mL
Contraindications — Cell Saver (High Yield)
  • Contamination by bacteria or sepsis
  • Contamination with bowel contents or urine
  • Contamination with amniotic fluid
  • Contamination with malignant cells (cancer)
  • Return of large cell saver volumes → coagulopathy risk (no clotting factors or platelets)

High Yield — ABO System

  • AB = Universal Recipient (no antibodies to attack any donor blood)
  • O negative = Universal Donor for emergency situations (no antigens)
  • Type & screen: predicts compatibility 99.94% of time
  • Type & crossmatch: predicts compatibility 99.95% of time
Rh System
  • ~85% of white individuals are Rh+; ~95% of Black Americans; ~100% of African individuals
  • Rh+ blood given to Rh– individual: anti-Rh agglutinins develop SLOWLY — first exposure may cause no immediate reaction
  • Second exposure: enough agglutinins already present → severe immediate reaction
  • Rh+ baby + Rh– mother → risk of erythroblastosis fetalis (hemolytic disease of newborn)
  • Hemolysis → bilirubin → jaundice, anemia, kernicterus (bilirubin on neurons → mental/motor defects)
  • Administration of RhoGAM to mother at ~30 weeks gestation helps prevent sensitization
Instructor Mnemonic

"Positive people can be around anyone and stay positive" — Rh+ can receive Rh+ or Rh–.
"Negative people only want to be around other negative people" — Rh– can ONLY receive Rh–.

Diagnosis of hemolytic reaction: free hemoglobin, low haptoglobin (binds Hgb), elevated bilirubin, positive Coombs test, hematuria. Give fluids to prevent renal tubule obstruction.

TRALI vs. TACO — Know the Difference

TRALI — Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury

  • Leading cause of transfusion mortality (43%)
  • Incidence: 1 in 5,000; Mortality: 10–20%
  • Criteria: Acute hypoxemia within 6 hrs of transfusion (PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤300 OR O₂ sat ≤90% on room air)
  • No evidence of left atrial hypertension (PAOP ≤18)
  • Mechanism: immune-mediated neutrophil activation → pulmonary endothelial injury → capillary leakage → bilateral infiltrates
  • 2-phase: Phase 1 = inflammatory response (neutrophil sequestration); Phase 2 = transfusion activates those neutrophils
  • Products implicated: FFP 49%, RBCs 29%, platelets 13%
  • Tx: optimizing oxygenation, maximal PEEP, low tidal volumes, avoid volume overload

TACO — Transfusion-Associated Cardiovascular Overload

  • Incidence: 5–10%
  • Criteria: Respiratory distress within 6 hrs of transfusion WITH evidence of left atrial hypertension
  • PAOP elevated (contrast with TRALI where PAOP ≤18)
  • Features: hypertension, tachypnea, tachycardia
  • Mechanism: simple volume overload — NOT immune mediated
  • NOT associated with increased capillary permeability
  • Tx: diuretic therapy and afterload reduction — responds well
High Yield — Metabolic Derangements with Blood Storage
  • As storage time increases: cellular components leak K⁺ and metabolize glucose → lactate
  • PRBCs contain >7 mEq/dL of K⁺ → ventricular arrhythmia or cardiac arrest. Greatest risk: renal insufficiency and neonates
  • RBCs undergo shape changes → impair microcirculatory flow
  • PRBCs have LOWER levels of 2,3-DPG and ATP → reduced O₂ carrying capacity
  • Citrate accumulation → chelates calcium → HYPOCALCEMIA (muscle weakness, tetany, arrhythmias, Trousseau's, Chvostek's, coagulopathy)
  • Acidosis usually cleared quickly by physiologic buffers — consider other causes if acidosis continues
  • Massive transfusion → metabolic ALKALOSIS from citrate metabolism to bicarbonate
  • Coagulation factor activity decreases 10% for every 1°C decrease in temperature — USE FLUID WARMER for rapid transfusion
  • Increased morbidity/mortality with blood stored >14 days

High Yield — Key Numbers
  • PRBC shelf life: ~40 days
  • FFP: stored up to 1 year. Discouraged if INR <2 without signs of bleeding
  • Coagulation not possible when factor levels <25% of normal
  • Platelets: stored at room temperature only 4–5 day shelf life — greatest bacterial contamination risk
  • Platelets: prophylactic when count <10,000; give when <50,000 with evidence of bleeding
  • Minimal fibrinogen ≥100 (normal ≥200 mg/dL); low fibrinogen strongly correlated with perioperative bleeding
  • Platelet dysfunction = MOST COMMON cause of post-op bleeding

Exam Tip — Connect Drug to Test
  • PT monitors WARFARIN (extrinsic + common) — checks Factors III, VII, X, V, II, I
  • PTT monitors HEPARIN (intrinsic + common) — checks XII, XI, IX, VIII, X, V, II, I, XIII

  • Normal platelet count does NOT ensure normal function
  • Prolonged bleeding time alone is NOT a reason to cancel surgery

PT.2 · 8 Coagulation Disorders (vWD, Hemophilia, Liver Disease, DIC)
▾
High Yield — Von Willebrand Disease

MOST COMMON inherited coagulation disorder (~1% of population affected; only ~0.01% symptomatic)

  • vWF functions: (1) facilitate platelet adhesion — attaches to GP1B receptors; (2) plasma carrier for Factor VIII
  • Synthesized in endothelial cells and megakaryocytes
  • Normal platelet count + bleeding → suspect vWF issue (adhesion problem)
  • Exhibit: prolonged bleeding time, qualitative deficiency and/or decrease in vWF and Factor VIII
  • Type 1 & 2 (mild/moderate): bleeding not likely unless surgery/trauma
  • Type 3 (severe): spontaneous bleeding (epistaxis, oral, GI bleeding)
TreatmentWhen UsedMechanism
DDAVP (0.3 mcg/kg IV over 10–20 min)First-line for Type 1 (mild forms)Stimulates release of vWF & Factor VIII from endothelial cells; augments platelet aggregation
CryoprecipitateSevere/unresponsive to DDAVP or current bleedingContains vWF, Factor VIII, Factor I, XIII
Recombinant Factor VIII-vWF concentrateModerate-severe diseaseReplaces vWF directly
High Yield — Hemophilia (X-Linked)
TypeDeficient FactorPrevalenceTreatment
Hemophilia AFactor VIII~1 in 5,000 males; 40% severeDDAVP or Factor VIII concentrate; FFP or cryo
Hemophilia BFactor IX (Christmas)~1 in 25,000Factor IX concentrate
Hemophilia CFactor XI~1 in 100,000FFP or Factor XI concentrate
  • Symptoms: spontaneous bleeding, muscle hematomas, pain at joint sites → decreased ROM → orthopedic intervention
  • Labs: prolonged aPTT; NORMAL PT and bleeding times
  • Ensure: type and screen even for low-risk procedures
  • Acquired hemophilia: connective tissue disorders, pregnancy, or malignancy → antibody production against Factor VIII
Liver Disease
  • Results in: thrombocytopenia, ↓ clotting factors made in liver, ↓ Protein C, S, and AT-III
  • Often have increased levels of Factor VIII and vWF (upregulation to maintain hemostasis)
  • Hemostatic equilibrium maintained but can easily tip toward bleeding OR coagulation
  • Conventional tests (aPTT, PT) do NOT reflect hematological tendencies — use TEG/ROTEM
  • Should NOT prophylactically transfuse without signs of bleeding
  • Still at risk for thrombosis — should receive prophylactic anticoagulation as indicated
High Yield — DIC (Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation)
  • Systemic coagulation disorder: systemic inflammation + coagulation activated by underlying pathology
  • Underlying causes: sepsis (most common), OB complications, ARDS, fat embolism, hypovolemia, massive transfusion, transplant rejection
  • Lots of tissue factor is released
  • Clinical presentation: Thrombosis AND/OR hemorrhage → multi-organ failure from widespread microvascular clotting depleting coagulation factors
  • Diagnosis: platelet count, fibrin degradation products, D-dimer, PT, fibrinogen
  • Treatment: Plasma is mainstay + platelets, FFP, cryo as indicated
  • Anticoagulation: important especially EARLY in DIC (in absence of major bleeding) — organ dysfunction often from CLOTTING

PT.2 · 10 Procoagulants & Reversal Agents
▾
High Yield — Antifibrinolytics (EACA, TXA, Aprotinin)

Mechanism: Competitively inhibit activation of plasminogen to plasmin — bind to plasminogen → block action of TPA and urokinase

DrugEvidenceKey RisksStatus
EACA (aminocaproic acid)Decreased bleeding; evidence less convincing than TXAN/V, headache, rare thrombosisAvailable
TXA (tranexamic acid)Strongest evidence for decreased bleeding; also inhibits thrombin at high dosesSeizures 1–4% (possibly GABA receptor blockade in frontal cortex); N/V, headache, rare thrombosis, vision changesAvailable; avoid in seizure disorder
AprotininPreviously usedIncreased death + kidney damage vs EACA/TXARemoved from US market

Most commonly used in major orthopedic procedures (total knee, total hip). Especially useful for mucosal bleeding (nose, oropharynx, GU tract — high fibrinolytic enzyme content).

High Yield — Protamine (Heparin Reversal)
  • Forms ionic bond with and NEUTRALIZES unfractionated heparin — ONLY agent available to do so
  • Does NOT reverse low molecular weight heparins
  • Protamine (protein base) + heparin (acid) = Chemical Antagonism (acid-base interaction)
  • Dose: 1 mg per 100 units of heparin given
  • Own anticoagulant effects: excess administration → inhibits platelets and serine proteases → avoid excess dosing
  • Heparin rebound: possible 2–3 hours after protamine (protamine t½ ~20 min; heparin t½ ~1 hr). Small dose 5–15 mg effective.
Clinical Warning — Protamine Side Effects
  • Pulmonary vasoconstriction, right ventricular failure, HYPOTENSION, anaphylaxis
  • Histamine release from mast cells → ADMINISTER SLOWLY
  • Anaphylaxis risk factors: fish allergy, NPH insulin use, prior protamine exposure, vasectomy (theoretical — made from salmon sperm; poor correlation in literature)
High Yield — Desmopressin / DDAVP
  • V2 analog of arginine vasopressin → stimulates release of vWF from endothelial cells
  • 3 Vasopressin Receptors: V1 = vascular smooth muscle constriction; V2 = stimulates ADH + vWF release + vasodilation; V3 = stimulates ACTH release
  • Shortens bleeding time in Hemophilia A (best for mild forms) and vWD (best for mild type 1)
  • Dose: 0.3 mcg/kg IV over 15–30 minutes to avoid hypotension (main concerning side effect)
  • Not useful in critically ill patients already receiving vasopressin (V2 receptors at max)
  • Side effects: MI, facial flushing, headache, water retention, hyponatremia

PT.2 · 11 Exam Quick Reference — All High Yield Points
▾

All high-yield points from the study guide, organized by topic for last-minute review.

Vessel & Platelet

  • Intima → Media (THROMBOGENIC) → Adventitia. Capillaries = intima only.
  • Adhesion (vWF+GP1B) → Activation (PLC+Ca²⁺) → Aggregation (GP2B/3A+fibrin)
  • White clot (primary) → Red clot (secondary, fibrin-strengthened)
  • ~7,100 platelets used daily for microvascular patching

Clotting Factors

  • Vit K: II, VII, IX, X ("1972")
  • NOT liver: III (tissue cells), IV (plasma)
  • No Factor VI. All active forms = factor + "a"
  • Factor IV (Ca²⁺) needed in ALL coagulation events

Coagulation Cascade

  • Extrinsic: III+VII+Ca → Xa. Fast (~30 sec).
  • Intrinsic: XII→XI→IX→VIII+Ca+phospholipids → Xa. Slow (min).
  • Common: Xa+Va+Ca → prothrombin activator (rate-limiting) → II→IIa (thrombin) → I→Ia (fibrin) + XIII → stable clot

Thrombin & Fibrinolysis

  • Thrombin activates: I, V, VIII, IX, X, XI, XII, XIII
  • 85–90% bound in fibrin. Thrombomodulin → Protein C → inactivates V & VIII
  • Major fibrinolysis: TPA + urokinase → plasminogen → plasmin → fibrin degradation
  • Heparin + AT-III → 1000× anticoagulant effect

O₂ & CO₂ Formulas

  • Bound O₂ = Hgb × 1.34 × O₂ sat
  • Dissolved O₂ = PaO₂ × 0.003
  • Dissolved CO₂ = PaCO₂ × 0.067
  • ETCO₂ ≈ PaCO₂ − 5 (normal)
  • 40-50-60/70-80-90 rule

Transfusion Triggers

  • Hgb <6 = almost always. >10 = rarely. 6–10 = clinical judgment.
  • Main reason = O₂ carrying capacity
  • ABL = EBV × (start Hct − accept Hct) / start Hct
  • Acceptable Hct ~25–30%. Hct ≈ Hgb × 3

Blood Components

  • PRBC: ↑Hgb 1, ↑Hct 2–3%. Hct 60–80%.
  • FFP: all factors + AT-III. 10–15 mL/kg. ↑coag 20–30%.
  • Plt: 1 unit/10 kg = ↑5–10k. Room temp. 4–5 day shelf life.
  • Cryo: I, vWF, VIII, XIII. 1 unit/5 kg = ↑fibrinogen ~100.

TRALI vs TACO

  • Both: within 6 hrs of transfusion
  • TRALI: PAOP ≤18; immune-mediated neutrophil activation; FFP #1 cause (49%); leading transfusion mortality; tx: PEEP + low TV
  • TACO: PAOP elevated; volume overload; tx: diuretics

Coagulation Tests

  • PT = warfarin (extrinsic+common; III,VII,X,V,II,I)
  • PTT = heparin (intrinsic+common; XII,XI,IX,VIII,X,V,II,I,XIII)
  • ACT = high-dose heparin in OR
  • PTT cannot detect factors 3 or 7

HIT Type II

  • IgG vs platelet factor 4. ANY heparin dose.
  • 50% drop or <100k platelets. HYPERCOAGULABLE.
  • ~30% mortality.
  • Tx: stop ALL heparin → argatroban.

Disorders

  • vWD = most common inherited coag disorder. DDAVP first-line (type 1).
  • Hemophilia: A=VIII, B=IX, C=XI. ↑aPTT, normal PT.
  • DIC: sepsis #1. Thrombosis AND hemorrhage. Plasma = mainstay.

Reversal Agents

  • Protamine: 1 mg/100 units UFH. Administer SLOWLY. Only reverses UFH.
  • Warfarin: Vit K (delayed), FFP (immediate), PCC
  • IIb/IIIa + fibrinolytics: DIALYSIS only
  • DDAVP: 0.3 mcg/kg IV over 15–30 min. Releases vWF. Best mild vWD/Hemo A.
